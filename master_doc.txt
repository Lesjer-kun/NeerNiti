0. Project Overview
0.1 Elevator pitch
Visarjan Ghat Watch turns historical water-quality measurements that are buried in long MPPCB reports into a ranked, 
evidence-backed briefing for the BMC officer planning Durga visarjan. It compares relevant readings with CPCB Class B 
criteria, keeps missing measurements visibly missing, traces displayed figures to their report pages, and prepares a short 
Hindi and English brief for operational planning.
0.2 Problem being addressed
MPPCB reports water-quality readings around Ganesh and Durga immersion at three stages: before immersion, during 
immersion and about one week afterwards. The supplied guide says these findings are published in reports roughly 87-
118 pages long, while the solution document describes reports of about 90-120 pages. The results are not conveniently 
aligned against the relevant criteria or compared across years for planning purposes.
The supplied documents highlight these findings for Bhopal ghats across 2023-2025:

Dissolved oxygen (DO) was reported as 0 mg/l four times at Bairagarh (Sant Hirdaram) kund across 2023 and 2025.

42 of 116 DO readings were below the CPCB Class B threshold of 5 mg/l.

100 of 112 BOD readings were above the CPCB Class B limit of 3 mg/l.
These are figures stated in the supplied project guide and solution specification. The build must reproduce the headline 
counts from the provided data before the dashboard relies on them.
0.3 Proposed solution
The project is designed to provide one focused workflow:
1. Rank seven Bhopal ghats by evidence-based risk, with a plain-language reason for each rank.
2. Let the officer inspect the measurements behind a conclusion, including year, festival, sampling phase, value, unit, flag 
and source PDF page.
3. Show data gaps directly so lack of sampling is not mistaken for good water quality.
4. Generate a short, shareable bilingual briefing with suggested testing, aeration and cleaning priorities, plus a clear 
limitations statement.
0.4 Target user and primary use case
Primary user: The BMC officer responsible for planning Durga visarjan at the ghats.
Primary use case: Before the festival, the officer reviews the ranked ghats, inspects the evidence and gaps, then uses 
the briefing to help prioritize where additional testing, cleaning or aeration should be considered.
The tool supports the officer's decision; it does not autonomously dispatch teams or make official regulatory or health 
determinations.
0.5 Core differentiator
Trustworthy, auditable briefing rather than an opaque AI answer. Python rules calculate metrics and risk tiers 
deterministically. An optional language model may draft explanations and Hindi text from a verified facts table, but it must 
not originate numerical claims. A checker verifies each number against the facts table and a phrase list blocks 
unsupported health claims.
0.6 Project objectives

1. Background and Problem Definition
1.1 Background
The MP Pollution Control Board (MPPCB) monitors water quality around Ganesh and Durga idol immersion. The readings 
cover a number of parameters and are recorded for phases before, during and after immersion. The source documents 
explain that a reader must interpret abbreviated parameter names, assumed units, missing markers and differing site 
labels, then compare values against applicable water-quality criteria.
The project guide also describes a planning context in which the National Green Tribunal (NGT) has banned immersion in 
the Upper and Lower Lakes and BMC is building dedicated ghats. The immediate planning milestone identified in the 
source documents is Durga visarjan on 20 October 2026.
1.2 Problem statement
A BMC officer planning Durga visarjan needs to decide where additional water testing, cleaning and aeration 
deserve attention, but the historic evidence is fragmented across lengthy reports and incomplete sampling records. 
Without a consistent comparison to stated limits and a visible account of missing data, the officer must manually 
reconstruct trends and may not know how strong or weak the evidence is for each ghat.
1.3 Current workflow described by the sources
Current workflow: MPPCB PDF reports → manual search for readings, years and phases → manual comparison across 
years → officer relies heavily on memory to prioritize action.
Proposed workflow:
1. Load the historical CSV, source reports and CPCB criteria.
2. Clean values while preserving the difference between missing, BDL and ND.
3. Calculate per-ghat metrics and evidence coverage.
4. Create a traceable facts table and deterministic ranking.
5. Draft plain-language reasons and Hindi text from verified facts.
6. Verify generated numbers and screen for unsupported health claims.
7. Publish the static dashboard, evidence view and bilingual brief.

1.6 Why this matters
The stated value is faster, more transparent planning from existing public evidence. The product should help the officer 
distinguish two different things: how badly readings breach selected limits, and how complete or incomplete the evidence 
is. It must not turn historical comparisons into unsupported claims about human health or assert that a ghat is safe solely 
because data is missing.

2.5 Existing workflow and opportunity
The baseline described by the sources is manual interpretation of lengthy reports, with decisions relying substantially on 
memory. The project opportunity is not to replace sampling; it is to structure existing evidence, show its limits, make 
source verification practical, and turn data gaps into explicit prompts for additional testing.
2.6 Evidence quality and research conclusions
The guide says the Bhopal CSV has been spot-checked against the source PDFs. The solution specification additionally 
requires the build's unit test to reproduce the headline counts and recommends manually spot-checking five to ten 
readings against the PDFs through source_page. These should be treated as verification tasks for each delivered build, 
not as evidence that the current implementation has already passed them.

3.5 Constraints and dependencies
The build depends on the supplied CSV files, the CPCB criteria file, report PDFs, Python and pandas, and a static-site 
workflow. If an LLM is used for prose, access to the selected inference provider is a build-time dependency, not a runtime 
dependency. Total coliform data is not available in the primary dataset according to the source guide.
4. Proposed Solution and Product Design
4.1 Core user experience
The product should open directly to a ranked overview, not a generic landing page or portal. The officer should see where 
risk indicators are concerning, why a ghat is positioned at that level, how much evidence exists and which important data 
gaps remain. Selecting a ghat should expose its evidence and the underlying source pages. A short brief should be 
available to copy or print.
4.2 MVP features
F1. Data and risk engine
Loads the Bhopal CSV, applies the missing-data rules, compares the supported parameters against Class B criteria, 
calculates ghat-level metrics, gives Durga data greater relevance for the planned Durga event, and outputs facts and 
source-page references. Metals are shown as readings only; suspended solids are not compared across years where the 
relevant Durga tables are absent.
F2. Ranked ghat view
Seven ghat cards, each with a risk tier, a one-sentence reason, evidence-strength badge and mini phase chart. Severity 
and evidence strength must be shown as two different dimensions. A ghat with few readings must not appear safe merely 
because few breaches were observed.
F3. Evidence and gaps drawer
A reading table with year, festival, phase, value, unit, flag and source page, plus a “What we don't know” panel listing 
missing years and phases. “Not sampled” and BDL use different visual treatments. The Rani Kamlapati / Kamla Park 
naming ambiguity must remain visible.
F4. Bilingual briefing
A one-page Hindi and English summary of the top-risk ghats, three action categories (testing, aeration and cleaning 
priorities) and limitations of the data. Provide copy-ready text and a printable PDF.

4.6 Product boundaries
The system calculates and presents historical evidence. It does not conduct sampling, validate real-time water conditions, 
issue regulatory determinations, predict health impacts, send crews, or establish that a site is safe. Suggestions about 
testing, aeration and cleaning remain decision support for the responsible officer.

5.3 Data flow
1. build.py reads the Bhopal CSV and applies missing-value rules while preserving the distinction between missing, 
BDL and ND.
2. The engine compares supported readings to CPCB Class B criteria and calculates ghat-level metrics.
3. The ranking logic produces severity and evidence-strength information separately; Durga readings receive greater 
weight for the planned Durga event.
4. A facts table stores values, calculated metrics and source-page links. This table is the approved numeric basis for 
explanations.
5. An optional LLM drafts reasons and Hindi text from that table. It is not allowed to originate numeric facts.
6. A number checker compares generated numbers with the facts table, while a phrase list screens against unsupported 
health claims.
7. The build emits static JSON and brief files, and the website displays them without a live backend.
The source specification does not define every internal formula or threshold for the overall risk tier. Those implementation 
choices must be explicitly documented, reproducible and tested rather than silently invented.

6. Technical Approach and Core Logic
6.1 Deterministic metrics and ranking
Risk computations must be handled in Python rather than generated by an LLM. For each ghat, the specification calls for:

Number of readings sampled.

Number of readings breaching the applicable limit.

Worst recorded value.

Trend by year.

Before/during/after sampling pattern.

A severity/risk view and a separate evidence-strength view.
Durga records are weighted more heavily because Durga visarjan is the event being planned; Ganesh data is supporting 
context. The supplied specification does not state exact numeric weights or the complete formula that converts metrics 
into each risk tier. The implementation must define that formula and document it alongside tests.
6.2 Handling values and flags
The parser must preserve three distinct states:
1. Numeric measurement available: evaluate the value against a threshold only where a threshold is defined.
2. BDL / ND: preserve the low/non-detected meaning and original flag; do not substitute zero.
3. Missing / not sampled: preserve the absence of a reading; do not count it as a compliant observation or present it as 
a clean result.
The evidence view should retain the original printed text to support manual source checks.
6.3 Evidence-strength calculation
Evidence strength is distinct from severity. The specification describes evidence strength in terms of the number of 
readings and which years/phases are missing. The precise formula is not supplied. A delivered build must state how 
coverage is summarized, handle ambiguous site mapping explicitly, and avoid making a sparsely measured ghat appear 
safer due to low observation count.
6.4 Test-Me-First logic
The planned secondary ranking identifies where an additional pre-festival test is most valuable. The source specification 
describes the score conceptually as severity x uncertainty: concerning historic readings combined with missing phases 
or no recent table should increase the value of testing. The exact scaling/normalization is not specified and must be 
documented before the ranking is used operationally.
6.5 Rank-stability analysis
The planned rank-stability control recalculates the ranking under alternate choices:

CPCB Class B versus Class C versus Class D limits.

Durga-only data versus both festivals.

With or without merging the Rani Kamlapati / Kamla Park records.
Ghats remaining in the top three across all tested settings may be labelled “robustly high-risk”; ghats whose ranks move 
should be described as assumption-dependent. Do not use these labels until the alternative scenarios have actually been 
calculated.
6.6 AI-assisted text and numeric audit
If an LLM is used:

Provide the verified facts table as the source for reasons and the Hindi brief.

Instruct it not to generate, calculate or infer new numbers.

6.7 Known analytical limitations

Total coliform is part of CPCB Class B criteria but is absent from the supplied dataset.

There is no Class B limit in the source criteria for chromium, lead, zinc or copper; show their readings without 
assigning a Class B pass/fail state.

Suspended solids are absent from the Durga 2023 and Durga 2025 tables, limiting cross-year comparisons for that 
parameter.

Some ghats have incomplete sampling phases or no recent Durga table.

The Rani Kamlapati / Kamla Park labels may refer to two places.

Historical readings do not establish the live water condition at the time of immersion.

The source documents do not provide the exact risk-tier formula, exact Durga-vs-Ganesh weights, or a validated 
forecast model. Do not imply these are supplied facts.

12. Impact, Value and Differentiation
12.1 Expected impact
The expected value, not yet a measured outcome, is that the BMC officer can review historic concerns and gaps faster 
and base follow-up decisions on traceable evidence rather than reconstructing information manually from multiple PDFs.
Potential benefits include:

Clearer priorities: a ranked view brings higher-concern sites into view.

Better testing decisions: gaps become explicit and can drive a “test first” recommendation.

More transparent reasoning: every ranking reason can be inspected against readings and limits.

More usable communication: a short Hindi and English brief can be forwarded to relevant teams.

Reduced risk of misleading output: deterministic calculations and numeric verification constrain AI-generated 
explanations.

Better institutional handover: public, readable code and reproducible artifacts support future maintenance.
No measured time savings, cost savings or environmental improvement are claimed in the source material.
12.2 Unique value proposition
For the BMC officer planning Durga visarjan, Visarjan Ghat Watch organizes historical MPPCB readings into a 
traceable, bilingual risk briefing that shows both where readings breach chosen limits and where the evidence is 
incomplete, helping the officer prioritize follow-up without treating missing data as clean.

13. Feasibility, Viability and Sustainability
13.1 Technical feasibility
The proposed implementation uses a compact batch pipeline (Python + pandas), generated JSON and a static website, 
with no runtime database or backend. This architecture is aligned with the narrow workflow and the event's one-day build 
constraint. Feasibility still depends on correctly implementing and testing parser semantics, rank logic, numeric auditing

13.2 Economic feasibility
The supplied documents do not provide a budget, hosting cost, inference cost or ongoing maintenance estimate. Static

the provider and estimating usage.
13.3 Operational viability
The intended user is narrowly defined, and the product is designed to open from a phone without installation or login. A 
short ranked view, evidence drawer and one-page brief fit the stated workflow. Adoption and actual usefulness still need 
to be confirmed by the BMC user or a domain expert; no user-acceptance result is included in the supplied documents.
13.4 Long-term sustainability
Maintenance depends on adding new public sampling data, validating site names and source pages, maintaining 
thresholds and methodology documentation, and rerunning the tests with each data update. The project should keep 
source data, calculation logic and generated outputs in a reproducible repository. Extension to new ghats or statewide 
data needs a separate data-quality pass.
13.5 Commercial viability
A commercial model is not specified and should not be forced onto this public-interest, open-source decision-support

17.3 Handover rule
A feature should be labelled implemented only when its code exists and a relevant check has passed. A feature in the 
solution specification but not demonstrated by the repository should remain planned or not verified. Ranking results 
should always carry the criteria/method context needed to understand them, and data gaps must remain explicit.
